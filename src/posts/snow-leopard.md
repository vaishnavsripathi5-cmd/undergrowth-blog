We sat in minus fifteen degrees for the better part of a week, scopes trained on a scree slope, before a shape finally moved that wasn't a rock.

You don't get the shot by being ready. You get it by still being there on the fourth day, after everyone reasonable has packed up and gone home.

The waiting is the part nobody photographs. Fingers going numb inside two pairs of gloves, the flask of tea gone cold within the hour, and a local spotter who never once seemed bored, because for him this wasn't waiting — it was reading a mountain he'd known his whole life.

When it finally happened, it lasted maybe forty seconds. A pale shape unfolding out of grey rock, moving like it wasn't spending any effort at all, and then gone again into a fold in the slope. Nobody said anything for a long moment afterward.

This is what that waiting actually feels like — and why it's worth it.
